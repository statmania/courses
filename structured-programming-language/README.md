**Structured Programming Language**
*   **Foundations:** Program structure, compilation process, variables, data types, operators, and type casting.
*   **Control Structures:** Conditional statements (if-else, switch), loops (for, while, do-while).
*   **Functions:** Function prototypes, parameter passing (by value/reference), recursion, variable scope, and storage classes.
*   **Arrays & Strings:** 1D/2D arrays, string manipulation, and standard library functions.
*   **Pointers:** Pointer arithmetic, pointers to pointers, dynamic memory allocation (malloc, calloc, free).
*   **Structures & Unions:** Defining custom data types, nested structures, bit-fields.
*   **File Handling:** File I/O operations, reading/writing text and binary files.

**Resources:**
*   **Textbook:** *The C Programming Language* by Brian W. Kernighan and Dennis M. Ritchie. Known as K&R, this is the definitive, concise guide to C.
*   **Textbook:** *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* (SICP) by Harold Abelson, Gerald Jay Sussman, and Julie Sussman. A classic MIT text focusing on abstraction and recursion.
*   **Online Course:** MIT 6.0001 *Introduction to Computer Science and Programming in Python* (MIT OpenCourseWare). Excellent for foundational programming concepts.
*   **Supplementary:** *Head First C* by David Griffiths and Dawn Griffiths for a more visual, beginner-friendly approach.

**Advanced Database Systems**
*   **Database Design:** ER modeling, Enhanced ER (EER), Relational mapping.
*   **Relational Model & Algebra:** Relational algebra, tuple/domain relational calculus.
*   **SQL & Advanced SQL:** Complex joins, subqueries, views, stored procedures, triggers, cursors.
*   **Normalization:** Functional dependencies, 1NF to BCNF, Multivalued dependencies (4NF), Join dependencies (5NF).
*   **Transaction Management:** ACID properties, concurrency control (2PL, timestamping, optimistic), deadlock handling.
*   **Storage & Indexing:** B-trees, B+ trees, hashing, RAID.
*   **Advanced Topics:** Distributed databases, NoSQL, Data Warehousing, OLAP, Query Optimization.

**Resources:**
*   **Textbook:** *Database System Concepts* by Abraham Silberschatz, Henry F. Korth, and S. Sudarshan. A comprehensive standard for both theory and practice.
*   **Textbook:** *Database Systems: The Complete Book* by Hector Garcia-Molina, Jeffrey D. Ullman, and Jennifer Widom. Covers design, use, and implementation in depth.
*   **Online Course:** Stanford Online *Databases: Advanced Topics in SQL* and *Databases: Modeling and Theory* (edX). Taught by Prof. Jennifer Widom.
*   **Textbook:** *Fundamentals of Database Systems* by Ramez Elmasri and Shamkant B. Navathe. A widely used text with strong coverage of design and implementation.

**Discrete Mathematics**
*   **Set Theory & Logic:** Sets, relations, functions, equivalence relations, Propositional and First-Order Logic.
*   **Proof Techniques:** Direct proof, proof by contradiction, mathematical induction, strong induction.
*   **Combinatorics:** Permutations, combinations, Pigeonhole Principle, Inclusion-Exclusion, generating functions.
*   **Recurrence Relations:** Solving linear recurrence relations, Master Theorem.
*   **Graph Theory Basics:** Graphs, subgraphs, isomorphism, connectivity, Eulerian and Hamiltonian paths.
*   **Trees & Boolean Algebra:** Spanning trees, Boolean functions, logic gates, Karnaugh maps.
*   **Number Theory:** Modular arithmetic, GCD, Euclidean algorithm, RSA basics.

**Resources:**
*   **Textbook:** *Mathematics for Computer Science* by Eric Lehman, F. Thomson Leighton, and Albert R. Meyer. The official text for MIT's 6.042J course, freely available online.
*   **Online Course:** MIT 6.042J *Mathematics for Computer Science* (MIT OpenCourseWare). Includes lecture videos, notes, and problem sets.
*   **Textbook:** *Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications* by Kenneth H. Rosen. A very popular and thorough textbook for undergraduate and graduate study.
*   **Textbook:** *Concrete Mathematics: A Foundation for Computer Science* by Ronald L. Graham, Donald E. Knuth, and Oren Patashnik. More advanced, focusing on the mathematical underpinnings of algorithms.

**Computer Graphics & Animation**
*   **Graphics Primitives:** Line drawing (DDA, Bresenham), Circle/Ellipse drawing algorithms.
*   **2D & 3D Transformations:** Translation, rotation, scaling, reflection, shear, homogeneous coordinates.
*   **Viewing & Clipping:** Window-to-viewport transformation, Cohen-Sutherland, Liang-Barsky, Sutherland-Hodgeman polygon clipping.
*   **3D Object Representation:** Polygon meshes, Bezier curves, B-splines, NURBS.
*   **Projections:** Parallel (Orthographic) and Perspective projections.
*   **Visible Surface Detection:** Z-buffer, Back-face detection, Painter's algorithm.
*   **Rendering & Animation:** Illumination models (Phong, Gouraud), shading, keyframing, morphing, procedural animation.

**Resources:**
*   **Textbook:** *Computer Graphics: Principles and Practice* by John F. Hughes, Andries van Dam, Morgan McGuire, et al. The authoritative, comprehensive reference for the field.
*   **Textbook:** *Computer Graphics: Theory and Practice* by Jonas Gomes, Luiz Velho, and Mario Costa Sousa. Provides a complete and integrated introduction.
*   **Online Resource:** ACM SIGGRAPH Education Committee. Offers curated lists of resources on animation techniques and graphics introductions.
*   **Textbook:** *Fundamentals of Computer Graphics* by Steve Marschner and Peter Shirley. A modern, accessible introduction focusing on core concepts.

**Advanced Data Structures & Algorithms**
*   **Algorithm Analysis:** Asymptotic notation (Big-O, Omega, Theta), amortized analysis.
*   **Advanced Trees:** AVL trees, Red-Black trees, B-Trees, Segment Trees, Fenwick Trees, Trie.
*   **Heaps & Hashing:** Binomial heaps, Fibonacci heaps, collision resolution techniques.
*   **Graph Algorithms:** BFS, DFS, Topological sort, Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall, MST (Prim's, Kruskal's).
*   **Algorithm Paradigms:** Divide and Conquer, Greedy, Dynamic Programming, Backtracking, Branch and Bound.
*   **Complexity Theory:** P, NP, NP-Complete, NP-Hard, reductions, approximation algorithms.

**Resources:**
*   **Textbook:** *Introduction to Algorithms* by Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest, and Clifford Stein (CLRS). The definitive, comprehensive reference for the design and analysis of algorithms.
*   **Online Course:** MIT 6.006 *Introduction to Algorithms* (MIT OpenCourseWare). Based on CLRS, with lectures and problem sets.
*   **Textbook:** *Algorithm Design* by Jon Kleinberg and Éva Tardos. Focuses on the design and analysis of algorithms with a more accessible approach than CLRS.
*   **Textbook:** *The Algorithm Design Manual* by Steven S. Skiena. A practical guide with a "war stories" section for real-world applications.

**Graph Theory**
*   **Fundamentals:** Degrees, handshaking lemma, walks, paths, cycles, connectivity, cut vertices and edges.
*   **Trees:** Properties of trees, spanning trees, minimum spanning tree algorithms, Cayley's formula.
*   **Eulerian & Hamiltonian Graphs:** Necessary and sufficient conditions, Fleury's algorithm, Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP).
*   **Matching & Covering:** Bipartite matching, Hall's marriage theorem, vertex cover, edge cover.
*   **Planarity:** Euler's formula, Kuratowski's theorem, dual graphs.
*   **Graph Coloring:** Vertex coloring, edge coloring, chromatic number, Four Color Theorem, chromatic polynomials.
*   **Network Flows:** Max-flow min-cut theorem, Ford-Fulkerson algorithm, Edmonds-Karp algorithm.
*   **Algebraic Graph Theory:** Adjacency matrices, incidence matrices, eigenvalues of graphs.

**Resources:**
*   **Textbook:** *Graph Theory* by Reinhard Diestel. A standard graduate-level text, available online for free.
*   **Textbook:** *Introduction to Graph Theory* by Douglas B. West. A widely used, comprehensive introduction for advanced undergraduates and graduates.
*   **Online Course:** *Graphs and Networks* by Coursera. Covers modeling real-world networks, graph basics, and representations.
*   **Textbook:** *A First Course in Graph Theory* by Gary Chartrand and Ping Zhang. A more accessible entry point for beginners.
*   **Lecture Notes:** *An Introduction to Graph Theory* by various authors on arXiv. Offers graduate-level introductions covering simple graphs, multigraphs, and directed analogues.
